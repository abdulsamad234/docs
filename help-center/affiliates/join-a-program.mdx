Every program you promote starts the same way: you find it, you work out whether it's worth your time, and you join. This guide covers all three — how to use the Referly marketplace to find programs, how to read a listing and its commission plan so you know what you'd actually earn, and what happens after you hit **Join program**.

## Where to find programs

The marketplace is a directory of affiliate programs running on Referly. There are three ways in:

- **From your Influencer dashboard.** Sign in at `influencers.referly.so` and select **Marketplace** in the left sidebar. This is the main way, and the only one that shows you personalized matches.
- **From any program portal.** Select the program button at the top of the left sidebar, then **Join other programs**. You'll be handed over to the marketplace without signing in again.
- **From the public marketplace.** Anyone can browse listings without an account. When you go to join one, you'll be asked to create your influencer account first, then dropped back on the listing you were looking at.

See [The affiliate portal](/help-center/affiliates/portal-overview) if you're not sure which of these you're looking at.

### Browse the marketplace home

The **Program marketplace** home page is built for scanning. It's organized into rows:

- **Recommended for you** — programs picked to fit your niches, audience, and earning history.
- **Most popular** — the programs affiliates are joining most.
- **New** — recently published listings, which often have less affiliate competition.
- **Content rewards** — programs paying for content you post, rather than only for sales. See [Content rewards](/help-center/affiliates/content-rewards).
- **Categories** — every category with published programs in it.

Select **View all programs** to leave the curated rows and search everything.

### Search and filter every program

The **All Programs** page is where you go when you know roughly what you want. Use the search box, then narrow with **Sort by** and **Filter**.

**Sort by** offers **Most popular**, **Newest**, **Name**, and **Relevance**.

**Filter** narrows on four things:

- **Category** — the niche the product sits in.
- **Business type** — the kind of company running it.
- **Reward type** — **Referral reward** (you're paid when someone signs up), **Sale reward** (you're paid when someone buys), or **Non-cash rewards** (coupons or store credit instead of money).
- **Commission rate** — how much the program pays.

A counter under the results tells you how many programs you're viewing out of the total, so you can tell whether your filters are too tight.

### Read a program card

Every card in the marketplace shows the same few signals, and learning to read them saves a lot of clicking:

- The **program name and logo**.
- A **Verified** badge. Verified programs run on Referly, which means tracking, rewards, and payouts all happen on the platform — so the numbers you see are the numbers you get. Unverified listings send you to the brand's own website instead.
- Your **match percentage** — how well the program fits you specifically.
- **Rewards** — a one-line summary of what the program pays.
- **Category**.

## Understand your match score

The match percentage on a card or listing is personal to you. Select it to open **Your match breakdown**, which shows the five things it's built from:

- **Niche match** — how closely the program's categories overlap the niches on your profile.
- **Proven affinity** — whether you already earn commissions in this category in your other programs.
- **Audience fit** — whether your audience size suits this kind of offer. Bigger audiences suit high-ticket and recurring offers; smaller ones suit broad, easy-to-sell offers.
- **Payout and geo fit** — whether the program can pay you with your preferred payout method, currency, and region.
- **Language fit** — whether the program's portal is available in your language.

Your score blends how relevant the program is to you (60%) with the program's own quality score (40%).

## Read a program listing

Opening a listing gives you the brand's full pitch. Work down the page:

- **The header** — cover image, logo, program name, the **Verified** badge, and your match score.
- **The one-line description**, plus tags for business type, category, and a link to the brand's website. Visit it. You're about to recommend this product to your audience.
- **Rewards** — a summary of what you're paid, with **View all** to open the full commission plan.
- **The brand's write-up** — who they're looking for, what converts, what they'd like you to promote.
- **Frequently asked questions** — often where the real terms live. Read them: payout timing, what counts as a valid referral, and any promotion rules tend to be answered here.
- **Content rewards** attached to this program, if there are any.
- **Related programs** — useful for comparing before you commit.

## Read the commission plan

Select **View full commission plan** to open **Commission Plan Details**. This is the part worth slowing down on, because two programs advertising "30%" can pay wildly different amounts over a year.

### What you're paid

Cash commission is either a **percentage** of the sale or a **fixed amount** per sign-up or per sale. A fixed amount is predictable; a percentage scales with basket size, which matters when the product has expensive tiers.

Some plans pay in **coupons** or **store credits** instead of cash. Those show as **Non-Cash Reward** and are worth checking carefully — a discount coupon is only worth something if you'd have bought the product anyway. See [Non-cash payouts](/help-center/affiliates/non-cash-payouts).

### When you're paid

The **Trigger** tells you what earns the commission: **Customer signs up** or **Customer makes a purchase**. Sign-up triggers pay more often but usually pay less each time.

There may also be a **Purchase condition** (first purchase only, or every few purchases) and a **Time window** — the customer has to buy within a certain number of months for you to earn.

### How long it keeps paying

This is the biggest difference between programs, and it's easy to miss:

- **Duration** — either **No time limit**, or a fixed number of months. After that, you stop earning from that customer.
- **Payments** — either **Unlimited**, or only the **first few payments** the customer makes.

A recurring 20% with no time limit will out-earn a one-time 50% on a subscription product, often within a few months.

### Whether the rate changes

Some plans say **Commission changes over time** and break the payout into an **Initial period**, a **Then**, and an **Ongoing** rate — usually higher at the start, lower later.

Others use **Milestones**: hit a set number of referrals or a revenue target and you're upgraded to a better commission plan. The dialog shows the requirements for each milestone and what you'd be upgraded to, so you can see the ceiling before you join. See [Performance bonuses](/help-center/affiliates/performance-bonuses) for the other way programs pay extra.

## Estimate what you would earn

Every listing has an earnings calculator in the right-hand panel showing **You can earn** an amount **per month**.

Drag the slider to the number of customers you think you could realistically refer each month, and the estimate updates.

Select the small info button to open **How we estimate your earnings**, where you can see and change the two numbers behind it:

- **Average customer value** — how much a customer typically spends. The label tells you where it came from: **from sales data** (calculated from that program's real sales, with the number of customers it's based on), **set by this program**, or an **industry benchmark** estimated from the category. A benchmark is a guess — if you know the product's actual pricing, type it in.
- **Commission rate** — set by the program, but editable if you want to model a different tier.

The math is deliberately simple: customers per month × commission per customer. **Reset to program defaults** puts back the original numbers.

<Warning>
  This is a model, not a promise. It doesn't account for how long a commission keeps paying, refunds, or how hard the product actually is to sell. Use it to compare programs against each other, not to plan your income.
</Warning>

## Join the program

When you've decided, select **Join program**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm the application">
    A dialog appears titled **Apply to** the program name. It tells you that your Referly profile information — your name, categories, social accounts, and payout details — will be used to apply.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Accept and continue">
    Your affiliate account for that program is created, your referral link is generated, and you're placed on the program's commission plan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Land in the program's portal">
    You're taken straight into the brand's affiliate portal in a new tab, already signed in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Because your details come from your existing profile, there's no second sign-up form and no second email verification.

### Approved instantly, or pending review

What happens next depends on a setting the brand controls:

- **Approved automatically** — your account is active right away. Copy your link and start promoting.
- **Pending review** — you'll see a screen asking you to wait while the brand reviews your application. You'll be told when they approve it, and the full portal unlocks then.

Either way, the program now appears in **My Programs** in your Influencer dashboard, and in the program switcher in every portal.

### What you get on day one

Once you're active, the program's portal gives you:

- Your **referral link**, ready to copy, shorten, or turn into a QR code. See [Your referral links](/help-center/affiliates/referral-links).
- A **promo code**, if the brand issues them. See [Promo codes](/help-center/affiliates/promo-codes).
- The brand's **marketing assets**. See [Marketing assets](/help-center/affiliates/assets).

## Join without the marketplace

Not every program is on the marketplace, and not every affiliate arrives through it.

**From the brand's own sign-up page.** Brands can share a direct link to their portal's sign-up page. You create an account there, and because the brand controls that form, you may be asked to answer **Additional Questions** they've set for applicants — how you plan to promote, your audience size, your website. Answer them properly; this is what the brand reviews when deciding whether to approve you.

**From an invite.** A brand can invite you directly. Follow the link in the invite and your account is already half set up.

Either route may also ask for your payout method and invoice details up front. See [Sign up as an affiliate](/help-center/affiliates/sign-up) and [Complete onboarding](/help-center/affiliates/onboarding).

Programs you join this way still show up in your Influencer dashboard, as long as you use the same email address everywhere. That's the one thing worth being disciplined about — one email keeps every program in one place.

## If you are declined

If a brand turns down your application you'll see **Your application was declined** when you try to open that portal. The brand made that call, and Referly can't overturn it. Contact the program owner if you think it's a mistake.

The most common fixable reasons are an empty profile, no connected social accounts, and application answers that don't say how you'd actually promote the product. Fill those in and you'll be approved more often — including by programs that approve automatically but review affiliates later.

See [Access issues](/help-center/affiliates/access-issues) for the other screens you might hit.
